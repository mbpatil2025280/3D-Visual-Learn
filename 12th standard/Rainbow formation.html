The Journey of Light

1. The Inciting Ray

A beam of pure, white sunlight travels from the Sun. White light may look simple, but it is actually composed of all the colors of the visible spectrum mixed together.

2. Initial Refraction (Bending)

The white light hits a water droplet. Water is denser than air, causing the light to slow down. Crucially, different colors slow down by different amounts. Short wavelengths (Violet) bend the most; long wavelengths (Red) bend the least.

3. Total Internal Reflection

The separated colors strike the back inner surface of the droplet. If the angle is just right, the light doesn't exit. Instead, it is reflected back inside the drop, like a mirror.

4. Secondary Refraction

The light reaches the front surface again and exits the droplet. It bends one last time, further spreading the colors apart. This wide spectrum is what travels towards your eyes!

The Final Effect

The droplet exits, sending a spectrum. Because thousands of droplets are doing this simultaneously, and each drop is at a unique angle relative to your eye, you perceive a beautiful, curved arc of colors.
