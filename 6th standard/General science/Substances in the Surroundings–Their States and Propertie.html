🧪 Substances in the Surroundings – Their States and Properties

States of Matter

All matter exists in one of three states: solid, liquid, or gas. The state depends on temperature and pressure.

💡 Water is the only common substance found naturally in all three states on Earth!

Solid

  • Has a definite shape and volume.
  • Particles are tightly packed and vibrate in place.
  • Examples: Stone, ice, wood, iron.

Liquid

  • Has a definite volume but no fixed shape (takes the shape of the container).
  • Particles are loosely packed and can flow past each other.
  • Examples: Water, milk, oil, mercury.

Gas

  • No definite shape or volume — fills its container completely.
  • Particles are far apart and move freely at high speed.
  • Examples: Air, steam, natural gas, oxygen.

Changes of State

  • Melting: Solid → Liquid (e.g., ice melts at 0°C).
  • Freezing: Liquid → Solid (e.g., water freezes at 0°C).
  • Evaporation/Boiling: Liquid → Gas (e.g., water boils at 100°C).
  • Condensation: Gas → Liquid (e.g., steam on a cold glass).
